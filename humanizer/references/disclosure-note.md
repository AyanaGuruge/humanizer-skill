# Drafting an AI assistance note

Many universities, journals and employers ask for a statement when AI tools helped with a text. The person decides whether one is needed; this file helps write an accurate one.

## Ask first (one question at a time, only what is missing)

1. Where will it appear (assignment cover page, acknowledgements, methods section, email to a manager)?
2. What did the AI actually do: brainstorm, draft, rewrite their own draft, correct language, summarize sources?
3. Does the institution or publisher prescribe wording? If so, use it exactly.

## Principles

- Say what was done, no more and no less. A rewrite of the person's own draft is not the same as AI generated content.
- Name the tool and the purpose; add the date if the policy asks for it.
- State that the person reviewed the result and is responsible for it.
- Do not claim the text is undetectable, and do not hide use that the rules require to be declared.

## Templates (adapt the brackets)

Language editing of the author's own draft:
> The author wrote the text. An AI assistant ([tool]) was used to edit language and style in a draft written by the author. The author reviewed all changes and takes full responsibility for the content.

Drafting support:
> An AI assistant ([tool]) was used to help draft parts of this text ([sections]) from the author's notes. The author revised the result, checked all facts and sources, and takes full responsibility for the final content.

Brainstorming and structure:
> An AI assistant ([tool]) was used for brainstorming and outlining. All text was written by the author.

Short version for an email or a form field:
> Edited with AI assistance for language; content and conclusions are my own.
